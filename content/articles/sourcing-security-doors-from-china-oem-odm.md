China manufactures most of the world's security doors, which means the sourcing question is never “whether” but “which factory, on what terms”. The gap between a good and bad Chinese supplier is enormous — and almost entirely detectable before you pay a deposit, if you know what to check.

## Factory vs Trader: The First Filter

- Ask for the manufacturing licence and the factory address — then verify they match the company you're paying
- Video-audit the production line, not the showroom
- Check for hard-to-fake substance: patents held, listed-company status, years in operation
- WONLY, for example, is a listed manufacturer (SSE: 605268) founded in 1996 with 1,000+ patents and multiple smart-factory bases — the kind of profile a trader cannot borrow

## Certification Due Diligence

Demand the actual certificates — GB 17565 grade for security, EN 1634 for fire, ISO 9001/14001 for the factory system, plus CE/UL and your market's approvals — and confirm each certificate's tested configuration matches the product quoted. A supplier who hesitates here will hesitate at every quality gate after payment.

## Structuring OEM/ODM Properly

- OEM (your brand on their platform): agree finish, packaging and branding specs in the contract, with approved pre-production samples as the standard
- ODM (co-developed product): settle tooling ownership, design exclusivity and minimum volumes in writing
- Lock the specification sheet — material thicknesses, lock configuration, cycle-test requirement — as a contract annex
- Define the inspection regime: pre-production sample, during-production check, pre-shipment inspection

## Protecting Quality to the Container

Quality fails at handoffs. Tie payment milestones to inspection results, specify export packaging for ocean freight, and confirm after-sales terms — warranty, spare parts and response time — before the first order, not after the first claim.
