For a distributor, developer or project buyer, choosing a security door is not about picking a look — it is about matching security, compliance, climate and supplier reliability to your market. Get it right and you win repeat orders; get it wrong and you inherit callbacks and warranty claims. Here is a practical, step-by-step buyer's guide.

## 1. Start With the Security Rating, Not the Price

The first question is how secure the door needs to be. Specify a verified burglar-resistance rating — the European EN 1627 RC classes or China's GB 17565 grades — instead of a vague “high security” claim. Residential low-rise usually calls for RC2–RC3; commercial and institutional work needs RC4 or higher. Always ask for the test certificate, not just a spec sheet.

## 2. Match the Material to the Climate and Market

- Cast aluminium — won't rust, near-zero maintenance, sculptural designs; ideal for coastal, humid and premium projects (Gulf, SE Asia, coastal Africa)
- Steel — maximum strength per cost; best for dry, sheltered, value-driven volume work

The wrong material in a salt-air market means rust bleed and returns, so match the metal to the environment before anything else.

## 3. Add a Fire Rating Where the Building Requires It

Hotels, hospitals, apartment corridors and public buildings usually mandate a fire rating alongside security. Confirm whether your project needs EN 1634 fire performance (for example 60- or 90-minute integrity) before you specify — retrofitting compliance later is expensive.

## 4. Check Certification for the Destination Market

A door that is compliant in one country may not clear customs or code in another. Confirm the door carries the certifications your market and insurer accept — commonly ISO 9001, CE, UL and regional fire/security approvals — and ask which export codes it already meets (Gulf, SE Asia, Central Asia).

## 5. Scrutinise the Lock and Hardware

The lock is the most-attacked point. Look for multi-point locking, an anti-drill / anti-bump cylinder, reinforced hinges, and hardware cycle-tested to a high count so it survives years of daily use.

## 6. Vet the Supplier, Not Just the Sample

- Track record — years in business, listed or audited status, patents
- Capacity — a real manufacturing base and output, not a trading company
- Range — can they cover residential, fire, commercial and medical in one order
- Support — certification packs, spare parts and after-sales
