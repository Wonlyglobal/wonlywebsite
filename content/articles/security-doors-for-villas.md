A villa entrance carries two jobs at once: it is the property's strongest security point and its most visible design statement. The best villa doors refuse to trade one for the other. Here is what to specify so you get both.

## Security: Specify a Class, Not an Impression

Villas are exposed — street-facing, often unattended, and a known target profile. Specify a verified rating (EN 1627 RC3 as the practical villa baseline, or GB 17565 Class A) with multi-point locking, an anti-drill cylinder and concealed anti-lift hinges. A heavy-looking door without a tested rating is decoration.

## Material: Cast Aluminium for Exposure

- Won't rust — holds its finish through salt air, humidity and rain
- Casts into sculptural, high-relief designs steel cannot match
- Near-zero maintenance across decades of ownership
- Pairs with a steel core for certified strength

This is why cast aluminium dominates premium villa work in the Gulf, Southeast Asia and coastal Africa — it is the material that still looks new at year ten.

Premium villa buyers increasingly expect the entrance to greet them: 3D face recognition, fingerprint and app unlocking, auto open-and-close for hands-free entry, a video peephole, and integration with the home system. Villas are rarely standard sizes. Confirm the manufacturer offers non-standard dimensions, double-leaf and pivot formats, custom finishes and glass inserts — real OEM/ODM capability rather than a fixed catalogue. ## FAQ

What security grade should a villa door have? RC3 (EN 1627) or GB 17565 Class A is the practical benchmark for street-facing villas; escalate for isolated or high-value properties.

Is cast aluminium as strong as steel? A rated cast-aluminium doorset with a steel core meets the same certified resistance classes — the rating, not the base metal, guarantees the security.
