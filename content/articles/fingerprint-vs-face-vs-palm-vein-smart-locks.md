Every biometric has a failure mode: fingerprints struggle with wet or worn skin, face systems must beat spoofing and backlight, palm-vein needs a deliberate gesture. That is why the comparison matters — and why the best answer for a family door is usually “more than one”.

## Head to Head

- Fingerprint — fastest habitual unlock; excellent for daily users; weaker with wet, dirty or age-worn prints; capacitive sensors resist fake-print attacks
- 3D face — completely hands-free at approach; best for arms-full arrivals and accessibility; requires depth sensing to resist photo spoofing; works in darkness via IR
- Palm-vein — reads vein patterns beneath the skin, unaffected by surface wear and extremely hard to spoof; slightly more deliberate gesture; strong for high-security and elderly users

## Match the Method to the Household

Young families lean on face unlock for pushchair-and-shopping arrivals; older users often find palm-vein more reliable than worn fingerprints; daily commuters default to fingerprint for speed. A multi-biometric lock lets each member use what works for them — which is exactly how return rates stay low for distributors.

## The Security Baseline Under Any Biometric

- Local, encrypted template storage — biometric data never leaves the lock
- Anti-spoofing tested for the specific sensor type
- False-entry lockout, pry and tamper alarms
- Non-biometric fallbacks: anti-peep PIN, RFID, app and mechanical key
- Cycle-tested hardware so the mechanism outlives the electronics hype

Which single biometric is most secure? Palm-vein is generally hardest to spoof; 3D face with proper depth sensing is close behind. For a family door, breadth of reliable methods matters more than a single champion.
