Ask a distributor in Lagos, Manila or Dubai what kills doors in their market and the answer is rarely burglars — it is weather. Salt-laden air, monsoon humidity and driven rain attack an entrance every day for decades. Material choice decides whether year ten looks like year one.

## How Coastal Air Destroys Steel Doors

Steel only survives outdoors through its coating. Every scratch, weld seam, drainage hole and hinge cut-out is a breach — and salt spray turns each breach into a rust site that creeps under the surrounding finish. The result is familiar across coastal markets: bleeding stains at the hinges within two or three years, then swelling, flaking and warranty calls.

## Why Cast Aluminium Is Different

- Aluminium does not rust — its oxide layer is self-healing and stable in salt air
- Casting produces deep, sculptural surfaces that hold premium finishes
- Roughly one-third the density of steel — easier handling and less hinge wear
- Near-zero maintenance: washing, not repainting

A cast-aluminium door is not “softer” security — the certified rating comes from the engineered doorset. ## What to Specify for Marine Exposure

- Cast-aluminium or equally corrosion-immune construction for any entrance within salt-air reach
- Stainless or coated hardware rated for marine environments
- Sealed, barrier-free threshold with a drop-seal against driven rain
- A finish warranty that names coastal exposure explicitly

## FAQ

How far inland does salt air matter? Corrosion studies commonly show elevated attack within several kilometres of the coast — and humidity extends the risk far beyond it. If the market is tropical or coastal, specify as if every door is exposed.
