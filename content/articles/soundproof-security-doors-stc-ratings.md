Buyers ask for a “soundproof” door; engineers deliver an STC number. Understanding the scale turns a vague comfort wish into a specification you can hold a supplier to — and it matters most in apartments, hotels and homes on busy streets.

## What STC Actually Measures

Sound Transmission Class rates how much airborne sound a partition blocks across the speech-frequency range — the higher, the quieter. As rough reference points: at STC 25 normal speech is easily understood through the door; at STC 35 loud speech is audible but not intelligible; above STC 40 loud speech becomes a murmur. Every 10-point gain is a large, clearly perceptible improvement.

## Where Door Sound Actually Leaks

- The gap under the door — the single biggest leak on most entrances
- Perimeter gaps between leaf and frame without continuous sealing
- A hollow, resonant core that transmits mid frequencies
- Letterplates, vision panels and hardware cut-outs

## How a Security Door Gets Quiet

The same construction that stops intruders helps stop noise: a dense, layered core; a tight, reinforced frame; continuous perimeter seals; and an automatic drop-seal that closes the under-door gap when the leaf shuts. WONLY doors combine a cast-aluminium shell with a honeycomb steel core, precision perimeter sealing and a sinking drop-seal threshold, achieving STC 35+ on applicable series — a level at which corridor conversation stops being your living-room soundtrack.

## Specifying Acoustic Performance

- Ask for a tested STC (or Rw) figure for the whole doorset, not a core-material claim
- Confirm the drop-seal and perimeter seals are included in the tested configuration
- For hotels and apartments, put STC 35+ in the tender line item

## FAQ

Does a higher security grade mean better acoustics? Often they travel together — denser cores and tighter frames serve both — but only a tested acoustic figure proves it. Specify both numbers separately.
