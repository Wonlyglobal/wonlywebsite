Ask a locksmith which part of a security door they attack, and the answer is never the slab — it is the cylinder. Most doors on the market, whatever the brand, share the same conventional figure-eight (“gourd”) cylinder: a soft brass body, a rotating face and an exposed keyway. That shared design is the industry's shared weakness.

## How Standard Cylinders Fail

- Snapping — grip the protruding brass body with pliers and twist; the cylinder breaks at its narrow waist
- Bumping and picking — a standard pin-tumbler keyway yields to bump keys and picks in minutes
- Drilling — brass offers little resistance to a cordless drill through the shear line

This is why forced-entry statistics are dominated by cylinder attacks: they are fast, quiet and tool-light.

## A Different Geometry: The Cylindrical Core

WONLY replaced the gourd format outright. Its patented cylindrical lock core — an industry-exclusive design — presents no gripping waist to snap, uses a hardened construction a drill will not bite into, and a mechanism whose resistance to technical (non-destructive) opening exceeds the top national requirement by a wide margin — up to 36 times the highest standard's threshold. In practical terms: locksmiths who advertise opening every lock routinely make an exception for this one.

## The Core in the System

A cylinder is only as good as the doorset around it. In WONLY doors the cylindrical core sits behind a reinforced escutcheon in a leaf with 16-bolt four-edge mechanical self-locking, certified to GB 17565 Class A and cycle-tested past 200,000 operations — so the strongest core in the industry is not compensating for weakness elsewhere.
