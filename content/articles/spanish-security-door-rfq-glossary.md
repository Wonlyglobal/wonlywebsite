A bilingual glossary helps a buyer ask consistent questions, but it cannot settle every regional construction term. Keep the English and Spanish pair together with a sketch and the project's intended meaning.

## Practical Review Checklist

- Door leaf — hoja: the moving leaf, not the complete doorset.
- Door frame — marco: identify the particular frame section and wall interface on the drawing.
- Clear opening — paso libre: show the intended usable passage and the agreed measurement condition.
- Door handing — mano de apertura: attach viewing side and swing arrow rather than relying on the word alone.
- Hardware schedule — cuadro de herrajes: connect the specified set to each door mark and revision.

## Record and Approval

Add a second group for evidence: test report — informe de ensayo; approved sample — muestra aprobada; drawing revision — revisión del plano; unresolved item — dato pendiente. These are working translations, not declarations that a term has one legally binding meaning across Mexico or all Spanish-speaking markets. Ask the local designer to confirm project usage. Send the resulting glossary with your RFQ so quotation questions and responses use the same definitions.

Send the door marks, quantities, destination and the completed review record to WONLY for a configuration-specific discussion. Confirm availability, evidence and responsibilities in writing; this checklist is not a product certificate or a universal installation instruction.

## FAQ

**Can this record replace the approved specification?** No. It organises the questions; the responsible designer, supplier and installer must resolve the actual configuration before release.
